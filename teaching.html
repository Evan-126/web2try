Teaching in the Makerspace

Training & Certification Process

For each method of manufacture in the Hello Maker Studio, including laser cutting, FDM 3D printing, SLA 3D printing, and CNC machining, users are required to complete a combination of online training modules and in-person certifications. These trainings are designed to introduce the fundamentals of each process, emphasize safety, and ensure that individuals can operate the equipment independently and responsibly.

Most other tools and processes offered in the Studio are supported primarily through online training and do not require formal in-person certification. That said, safety is always prioritized. Staff members are available to walk individuals step by step through the use of any tool or method they may be unfamiliar with, such as soldering or drilling, and will actively supervise first-time use when needed.

Unfortunately, I cannot share direct links to the training courses with anyone outside of UT Austin. However, anyone interested is welcome to stop by the Studio and scan our QR code to access all required forms and training materials.

Writing and Teaching Online Training Modules

One of my roles as a mentor is contributing to the online training modules used in the makerspace. This includes adding my expertise to preexisting trainings as well as writing entirely new modules from scratch.

These modules are designed to function both as an initial introduction to a machine—assuming the trainee may have never encountered the process before (such as CNC machining)—and as a comprehensive reference during active use. While students and faculty are always encouraged to ask staff for help, each module must independently provide all necessary information, along with tools and resources for finding additional details if needed.

In-Person Trainings

I routinely conduct in-person trainings for 3D printers and laser cutters. While the CNC machine is less frequently requested, I have begun running staff members through the full process as part of expanding internal expertise.

CNC Machining

Overview

Makerspace got a makera carvera (cnc machine shown in Fig 2.1) and I was tasked with setup, trial runs, and producing a teaching module on canvas and in person to bring anyone who wants to up to speed on everything about the CNC.

Figure 2.1

Writing the Online Training

This includes general safety, what materials are acceptable, what tools we have, the whole concept of feeds and speeds, how to prepare a preexisting CAD module and generate toolpaths from it, which tools are best for which operations, exporting gcodes, ensuring there won’t be any conflicts in the work envelope once the job starts, and one final safety briefing. Of course there are a multitude of smaller steps involved within each one of these points, but the main targets have all been hit.

Figures 2.2 and 2.3 show a list of all the pages involved in this training, and an example of what one of the pages looks like.

General Logisticcs

There were also parts of creating this module that the students will never have to worry about. For example, I had to make sure the Training CAD model was relatively small (so the training would take a less copious amount of time) and simple (so complications with the file itself were minimized), while also making sure to utilize as many features of the CNC as possible – primarily automatic tool changing.
