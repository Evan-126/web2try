Teaching in the Makerspace

Training & Certification Process

For each method of manufacture in the Hello Maker Studio, including laser cutting, FDM 3D printing, SLA 3D printing, and CNC machining, users are required to complete a combination of online training modules and in-person certifications. These trainings introduce the fundamentals of each process, emphasize safety, and ensure individuals can operate equipment independently and responsibly.

Most other tools in the Studio are supported primarily through online training and do not require formal in-person certification. Staff members are always available to walk users step by step through unfamiliar tools and will actively supervise first-time use when needed.

Unfortunately, I cannot share direct links to the training courses with anyone outside of UT Austin. Visitors are welcome to stop by the Studio and scan our QR code to access required materials.

Writing and Teaching Online Training Modules

One of my roles as a mentor involves contributing to the online training modules used in the makerspace. This includes expanding preexisting modules and writing entirely new ones from scratch.

Each module serves both as an initial introduction to a machine and as a comprehensive reference during use. Modules must independently provide all necessary information while also directing users to additional resources when appropriate.

In-Person Trainings

I routinely conduct in-person trainings for 3D printers and laser cutters. While CNC trainings are less frequently requested, I have begun running staff members through the full process to expand internal expertise.

CNC Machining

The makerspace acquired a Makera Carvera CNC machine (Figure 2.1), and I was tasked with setup, testing, and developing both online and in-person training materials to bring users fully up to speed.

Writing the Online Training

The CNC training covers safety, acceptable materials, tooling, feeds and speeds, CAD preparation, toolpath generation, G-code export, work envelope verification, and a final safety briefing.

General Logistics

I also handled behind-the-scenes logistics, such as designing a compact training CAD model that minimized run time while still demonstrating key features like automatic tool changing.
